The harbour that keeps giving the world its voice

Every crossing to Santo Antão begins in Mindelo, and Mindelo has always been a place of leaving and returning. For generations this harbour on São Vicente sent its people out across the ocean to build lives abroad, and it taught them sodade — the Cape Verdean word for the ache of longing for a home you have left. That feeling is the heart of morna, the islands' blues, and no one ever carried it further than a barefoot woman born here in 1941.

Cesária Évora grew up poor in Mindelo, lost her father young, spent time in an orphanage, and started singing at sixteen in the port's taverns for sailors and small change. Recognition came slowly and then slipped away entirely; through the 1970s she stopped singing, her own "dark years." She did not record her first international album until she was forty-seven, and became a global star only in her fifties — touring the world barefoot, a Grammy winner, the Queen of Morna who made a tiny nation's music something the whole world could feel. She always came home to Mindelo, where she is buried and where the airport now carries her name.

Half a century later the same small harbour produced another late arrival to the world stage. Vozinha, born in Mindelo and brought up through the local clubs Batuque and Mindelense, did not turn professional until his mid-twenties, then carried his career across Angola, Moldova, Portugal, Cyprus and Slovakia. At forty, keeping goal for Cabo Verde at the country's first-ever World Cup, he shut out Spain and was named the game's best player — the oldest footballer ever to appear in a nation's World Cup debut. A country of barely half a million people, one of the smallest ever to reach the tournament, had the world watching, and one of its own from this port stood in the middle of it.

Two children of the same harbour town, fifty years apart: both made to wait, both patient, both carried out across the world and back, both bringing the name of these islands somewhere new. That is Mindelo — and it is why the town is worth a day of its own before you sail.

What São Vicente is

São Vicente is small, dry and mountainous, and almost everything happens in Mindelo, its one real town, wrapped around a deep natural harbour on the island's north coast. It is widely thought of as the cultural capital of Cabo Verde: the home of morna and coladeira, of a lively music and bar scene after dark, and of the country's most famous Carnival, held each February in a burst of colour, drums and costume. The waterfront, the old customs house and the pastel colonial buildings make the centre easy and pleasant to walk. Out of town, the island opens into quiet beaches and headlands — the fishing village of São Pedro near the airport, the sands at Praia Grande and Salamansa, the summer music festival on the bay at Baía das Gatas (back 6–9 August 2026 — ferry advice here), and the green heights of Monte Verde looking back over the harbour.

Getting to São Vicente

Most visitors arrive one of two ways. By air, into Cesária Évora Airport just outside Mindelo, which links São Vicente with other islands such as Santiago and Sal and carries some regional international flights. By sea, on the ferry from Santo Antão, the short, busy channel crossing that runs several times a day in each direction between Porto Novo and Mindelo. If you are island-hopping, Mindelo is one of the network's main hubs, and the sea route to Santo Antão is the one you will use most.

The crossing to Santo Antão

São Vicente has no mountains to rival its neighbour, and that is the point: Santo Antão, the greenest and most dramatic of the islands, has no airport at all, so the ferry from Mindelo is the only way to reach it. The crossing itself is short, roughly an hour depending on the sea, and two operators run it, Nôs Ferry and CV Interilhas, between them giving several departures a day. Because sailings and fares change and the schedule is only published a short way ahead, the honest advice is to check the live times rather than trust a fixed timetable, and to arrive at the terminal thirty to forty-five minutes early.

When to go

The sea between São Vicente and Santo Antão is calmest and most reliable from roughly April to October, and roughest in the winter months of November to March, when the Atlantic swell can make the channel lively — worth knowing if you are prone to seasickness. February is Carnival, the single biggest event on the island's calendar and a wonderful, busy time to be in Mindelo if you plan ahead. Whenever you come, give the town a day of its own: walk the waterfront where Cesária sang, feel the morabeza the islanders talk about, and then let the port do what it has always done and carry you on across the channel.

Main town: Mindelo
